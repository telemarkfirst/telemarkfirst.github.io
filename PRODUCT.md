# Telemark

## Register

product

The dominant surface is a learning application: 130 lessons, 12 calculators,
26 quizzes, a progress dashboard. Design serves the content. The homepage is
the one brand surface and is treated as such.

## Users

FTC robotics team members, roughly ages 12 to 18, plus their mentors.

Two distinct audiences that must feel equally served:

- **Programmers** working through Java, the FTC SDK, and autonomous routines.
- **Builders** working through the design process, CAD, transmission,
  mechanisms, wiring, and fabrication.

They read on school Chromebooks and phones, often in a noisy workshop between
build tasks, sometimes at a competition in a loud venue with minutes to spare.
Many are reading their first technical documentation of any kind.

## Product purpose

Teach both halves of building a competition robot, and make it obvious that
they are two views of one machine. A student should be able to follow a gear
ratio from the lesson where it is chosen to the lesson where the code divides
by it.

## Brand and tone

Precise, plain, and unpatronising. The voice of a good mentor: states the
mechanism, names the failure mode, gives the number. Never hype, never
"awesome", never exclamation marks.

Written by students for students, which shows up as candour about failure.
Lessons say what breaks and why, not just what works.

## Anti-references

- **Bootcamp marketing.** No countdown urgency, no testimonials, no
  "transform your team" copy.
- **Gamified learning apps.** No streaks, badges, confetti, or mascots.
  Progress is recorded because it is useful, not to manufacture habit.
- **Generic SaaS documentation.** No identical icon-heading-paragraph card
  grids, no stock illustration.
- **Hacker-cosplay dashboards.** The terminal motif is one accent, not the
  whole personality. No fake scanlines, no matrix rain, no glitch effects.

## Strategic principles

1. **Reading is the job.** Long-form technical prose is the primary content.
   Anything that competes with the text loses.
2. **Two tracks, one product.** A student switching tracks must meet the same
   furniture: same landing shape, same assessment, same navigation. Content
   differs; chrome does not.
3. **Interaction must teach.** Calculators, simulators, and animation exist to
   make a relationship visible. Decoration that teaches nothing is removed.
4. **Honest about limits.** The site says what it cannot tell you: a simulator
   is not a robot, a calculator is not a prototype, a diagram is not a photo.
5. **Works on a school Chromebook.** No effect worth a dropped frame on
   low-end hardware, and every animation yields to reduced motion.
